# Tech Lead CV Generation Brief

## Objective

Generate a concise, ATS-friendly resume positioning Davis primarily as a **Technical Lead / hands-on engineering leader**.

The resume must communicate that his career has progressed from data-intensive and full-stack engineering into architecture, technical ownership and team leadership **without moving away from hands-on software development**.

The candidate should not be positioned primarily as:

- a CTO/executive  
- a people manager  
- a data miner  
- a business strategist  
- a generic full-stack developer  

Those are parts of his experience, but the central professional identity is:

**Technical Lead with strong hands-on full-stack engineering, architecture and product ownership experience.**

---

## Career Story

The resume should make the following progression easy to understand:

**Data acquisition and backend engineering → full-stack engineering → platform and performance engineering → cross-team technical ownership → technical leadership and architecture ownership.**

Earlier roles establish the technical foundations.

Recent roles demonstrate increasing ownership, architecture, mentoring, leadership and business awareness.

The resume does not need to explicitly narrate this progression. It should emerge naturally through the ordering and selection of evidence.

---

## Primary Positioning

Position the candidate as someone who can:

- lead engineers technically  
- make architecture and technology decisions  
- remain hands-on in production code  
- own technical roadmaps and delivery  
- mentor engineers  
- modernise legacy systems  
- work across frontend, backend, data and infrastructure  
- diagnose and solve difficult performance problems  
- collaborate with Product, Design, Sales, Customer Success and company leadership  
- understand the business implications of engineering decisions  

Leadership must strengthen the engineering profile rather than replace it.

---

## Current CTO Title

The candidate currently holds or has progressed into a CTO title at a small company.

Do not hide or falsify this title.

However, do not position the candidate as a traditional executive CTO.

Represent the role using wording such as:

**Technical Lead → CTO (Hands-on Engineering)**

The role should clearly communicate that the candidate continues to:

- design systems  
- make architecture decisions  
- develop software  
- migrate legacy code  
- solve technical problems  
- mentor engineers  

The main resume headline should remain oriented toward **Technical Lead**, not CTO.

## Professional Summary

Generate a short professional summary near the top of the resume.

Aim for approximately 50–80 words and 2–4 sentences.

The summary should establish:

- roughly 10 years of professional experience, calculated from the supplied dates  
- Technical Lead positioning  
- hands-on engineering background  
- architecture and technical ownership  
- full-stack experience  
- mentoring/leadership  
- one or two especially relevant technical strengths  

Do not write a personality biography.

Avoid unsupported or generic claims such as:

- passionate  
- ambitious  
- hard-working  
- visionary  
- exceptional  
- rockstar  
- ninja  
- quick learner  
- results-driven  

Demonstrate qualities through evidence instead.

---

## Headline

Use a conventional, searchable professional headline.

Prefer:

**Technical Lead**

or, when additional context genuinely helps:

- Technical Lead | Full-Stack Engineering  
- Technical Lead | Software Architecture & Full-Stack Engineering  

Keep the headline concise.

Do not use inflated branding language.

Do not put CTO as the primary headline.

---

## Education
Keep education concise and secondary to professional experience.

Use the exact supplied qualification title, institution and dates.
Never infer or rename academic qualifications.

At this career stage, normally use 1–2 lines for education.

The bachelor’s thesis may be included when relevant to the target role,
particularly for positions involving AI, machine learning, data or
technical experimentation.

Do not include:
- academic struggles
- generic descriptions of university life
- personality reflections
- irrelevant coursework
- unsupported grades

---

## Work Experience Writing

Select facts rather than attempting to reproduce all available source material.

Recent and important positions should receive substantially more space than older positions.

Prefer:

**achievement / technical action + scope + measurable outcome**

over descriptions of general responsibility.

Preserve genuine numbers and metrics.

Never invent:

- metrics  
- technologies  
- team sizes  
- responsibilities  
- customer numbers  
- system scale  
- performance improvements  
- promotions  
- dates  

Use factual, active language.

Avoid first-person prose.

Avoid anecdotes, philosophical reflections, career lessons and commentary.

## Skills

Do not include every technology the candidate has ever used.

Prioritise technologies that are:

1. relevant to Technical Lead positions  
2. supported by the candidate profile  
3. current or strongly demonstrated  
4. requested by the job description when one is supplied  

Prioritise the candidate's strongest modern stack and technical leadership capabilities.

Older technologies may be omitted unless relevant to the vacancy.

Do not keyword-stuff.

A technology used within an experience bullet is generally stronger evidence than the same technology appearing only in the Skills section.

---

## Job Description Tailoring

When a target job description is provided:

1. identify its most important technical and leadership requirements  
2. identify terminology used repeatedly by the employer  
3. find genuine candidate facts that support those requirements  
4. prioritise those facts in the summary, skills and experience bullets  
5. use the employer's terminology where it accurately describes the candidate's experience  

The job description controls **relevance and wording**, not truth.

Never add a technology, responsibility or accomplishment solely because it appears in the job description.

If the vacancy asks for a skill unsupported by the candidate data, omit it.

---

## ATS and Human Readability

The resume must work for both automated parsing and rapid recruiter scanning.

Use conventional section names.

Keep language concise and specific.

Use relevant technical keywords naturally.

Prefer measurable evidence.

Avoid decorative or clever terminology.

Do not sacrifice human readability for keyword density.

---

## Length

Target approximately two pages after rendering.

The resume should feel selective.

Its purpose is not to document the candidate's entire career.

Its purpose is to provide enough strong evidence to earn a Technical Lead interview.

If information does not materially strengthen that goal, omit it.
